## Why you are receiving this

Thank you for the direction you gave us on 19 August. It reshaped the platform around one principle: **digitize the back office, not the relationship.** You stay at the centre of every client interaction. The software works behind you.

We are now preparing the **pilot**: a small, controlled first use of the platform with a limited number of clients. For the pilot we want to build the **simplest working version** of each feature, confirm it with you, and add depth after the pilot proves itself.

The platform is built for how you work today: **one person running a premium service.** Every feature aims to take weight off your day to day, so the system carries the routine work and you keep the judgment calls. It is also ready to grow with you: when you eventually bring in a team, the team features get built then. Where a question looks ahead to that day, it is marked **for later**.

This questionnaire makes sure the pilot contains exactly what you need, nothing more and nothing missing. Your answers become our agreed requirements. They also give us a baseline, so if anything changes during development we can record the change, agree it, and date it instead of losing it in conversation.

**Time to complete: about 45 minutes.**

## How to answer

Every feature is marked with one of three priority levels:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **MUST** | The pilot is not a success without it. Build first. |
| **SHOULD** | Genuinely valuable, but the pilot still works without it. |
| **LATER** | Agreed and recorded now, built after the pilot. |

If you are unsure, mark it SHOULD. Nothing marked LATER is forgotten; it goes on the after-pilot list with a date.

## How to run the answers once returned

1. Transfer every answer into the requirements register as UR-IDs (UR-A1, UR-B2, and so on). Each UR records: the answer, the date, and the source (questionnaire, call, or message).
2. Build the system requirements for the pilot by walking the priority grid: every MUST becomes a system requirement traced to a UR and to the 30-day doc section above. Every SHOULD becomes a stretch item. Every LATER goes to the after-pilot backlog with an owner.
3. During development and the pilot, every new ask becomes a CR-xx entry in the change log, linked to the UR it modifies. Review the change log on the weekly call agreed in H4. A CR approved as "now" updates both the system requirements and the UR; a CR approved as "after pilot" moves to the backlog.
4. The signed questionnaire (Section I sign-off) doubles as the signed scope decision required by Days 1 to 2 exit criteria.
5. Watch for MUST answers that conflict with the go-live date of 18 September. Each conflict goes to Ms Bolanle with a cost in days, not silently absorbed.
6. Provision exactly one advisor account (Ms Bolanle). Keep the permission model and row-level isolation in the codebase for the future team, but spend no pilot effort on multi-advisor workflows.
